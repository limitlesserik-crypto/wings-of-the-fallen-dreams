A TALE OF LOVE AND SACRIFICE

The Weight of Being the Eldest

The morning sun had barely risen when Nat opened his eyes. His alarm rang at five in the morning, but he had already been awake for several minutes. It was another day of responsibilities, another day of pretending that everything was fine. At twenty years old, Nat was already carrying a burden that most people his age could hardly imagine. He was a college student, a part-time worker, and, most importantly, the only person keeping his family financially alive. Their small house was far from perfect. The roof leaked whenever it rained, the walls were old, and the kitchen was almost always empty. Yet Nat continued to call it home because it was where his younger siblings lived. As he entered the kitchen, he found Ella sitting at the table, quietly waiting for breakfast. "Good morning, Ate—" Ella stopped and giggled. "Good morning, Kuya Nat!" Nat smiled. "Good morning, little one. Did you sleep well?" Ella nodded. "Kuya, can I ask you something?" "What is it?" "Can I go to school tomorrow? I want to see my friends." Nat paused. Ella's illness had made her miss several school days. Her medication was becoming increasingly expensive, and the family could barely afford her treatment. "Of course, Ella. I'll see what I can do." Ella smiled brightly. "Thank you, Kuya! You're the best brother in the world." Those words were enough to make Nat forget, even for a moment, how tired he was. But when he entered the living room, the peaceful morning quickly disappeared. His father was lying on the couch, watching television. His mother was sitting nearby, scrolling through her phone. "Mom, here's the money for Ella's medicine," Nat said, placing a few bills on the table. "Please make sure you buy them today. The doctor said she shouldn't miss her medication." His mother barely looked at him. "Yes, yes. I know." "Please, Mom. That's the money I earned yesterday. It's important." His father suddenly spoke. "You're making too much noise this early in the morning." Nat looked at him. "Father, could you at least help us? I can't keep doing everything alone." His father frowned. "You're the eldest. It's your responsibility to help this family." Nat wanted to answer, but he knew another argument would only waste his time. He took a deep breath. "Fine." He grabbed his bag and headed for school. Behind him, Ella watched him leave. "Kuya, don't forget to eat!" Nat turned around and smiled. "I won't. You take care of yourself, okay?" He didn't tell her that he would probably skip breakfast again. He didn't tell her that the money in his pocket was barely enough for transportation. And he certainly didn't tell her that he was beginning to wonder how much longer he could continue living this way.
