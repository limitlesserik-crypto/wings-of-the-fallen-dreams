A TALE OF LOVE AND SACRIFICE

Wings Of The Fallen Dreams

CHAPTER 06 — THE END

The house had never felt so empty. It had been several weeks since Ella's funeral, but Nat still couldn't accept that his little sister was gone. Every morning, he would wake up expecting to hear her voice. "Kuya Nat! Good morning!" But there was nothing. Her little shoes remained beside the door. Her schoolbooks were still on the table, and the drawing she had given him was still inside his notebook. Everything remained where she had left it. Except Ella. Nat continued going to work and attending school, but something inside him had changed. He no longer laughed with his friends. He barely spoke to anyone, and the dreams he once had seemed to have disappeared. One afternoon, John approached him outside the classroom. "Nat, are you coming with us?" "No. I have work." "You always have work. When was the last time you actually rested?" Nat forced a smile. "I'm okay, John." John looked at him seriously. "You're not okay. You haven't been yourself since Ella passed away." Nat looked down. "Every time I close my eyes, I remember her." He paused. "I keep thinking about all the things I could have done differently. Maybe if I had earned more money, maybe if I had worked another shift, maybe if I had noticed her condition earlier..." "Nat, stop blaming yourself. You did everything you could." "But she trusted me, John." His voice cracked. "She believed I could protect her. I promised her that everything would be okay." John placed a hand on his shoulder. "You were her brother, Nat. You weren't supposed to carry the entire world on your shoulders." Nat said nothing.

⬇

That evening, Nat returned home and found his mother sitting on the couch. "Nat, do you have any money?" He stopped walking. "What?" "I need some money." Nat stared at her in disbelief. "Mom, Ella just died." His mother looked irritated. "What's that supposed to mean?" He slowly shook his head. "Even after everything that happened, you're still asking me for money?" "I have my own needs, Nat!" Nat laughed bitterly. "My own needs?" He looked around the house. "Where were your needs when Ella needed her medicine? Where were you when I was working until midnight just to keep this family alive?" His mother remained silent. Nat continued. "I wasn't asking you to give me a comfortable life. I wasn't asking you to buy me anything. I just wanted you to help me save your daughter." His eyes filled with tears. "But now she's gone." He walked toward his room. His father was sitting in the living room, saying nothing. Mark approached Nat. "Kuya..." Nat stopped. "I'm sorry." Nat looked at him. "For what?" "For not helping you before. I thought you were just being strict with me. I didn't understand how much you were carrying." Nat looked at his younger brother for a moment. Then he gently placed a hand on his shoulder. "Take care of yourself, Mark." It was the only thing he could say.

⬇

The following days became increasingly difficult. Nat stopped attending some of his classes. His professor repeatedly tried contacting him. One afternoon, the professor finally found him sitting alone outside the school building. "Nat." He stood up. "Good afternoon, Professor." "Why haven't you been attending your classes?" "I'm sorry, Professor. I've just been dealing with some things." The professor studied his face. "I know about your sister. I am truly sorry." Nat nodded. "I don't know what I'm supposed to do anymore, Professor." The professor remained quiet. "All my life, I've been working toward something. I wanted to graduate, find a good job, and give my family a better life. Most importantly, I wanted Ella to get better." He swallowed. "Now that she's gone, everything feels different." "Nat, your dreams still matter. Your life still matters. You deserve a future beyond the responsibilities you've carried." Nat looked away. "I don't know if I can do it anymore." The professor became concerned. "Then don't face this alone. Please let someone help you." Nat nodded, but his expression remained distant.

⬇

That night, John received a message from Nat. John, thank you for everything. You were the only person who listened when nobody else did. I'm sorry I couldn't be the friend you deserved. John immediately called him. But Nat didn't answer. He called again. Still nothing. John became worried and contacted Mark, asking him to check on his brother and make sure he wasn't alone.

⬇

The next morning, the family discovered that Nat was missing. His phone was left inside his room. Beside it was his old notebook. On the last page was a letter addressed to his family. It wasn't a detailed goodbye, but an expression of everything he had kept inside for years. He wrote about the exhaustion of being the only provider, the disappointment of having parents who wouldn't help, and the dreams he had once shared with Ella. He also wrote about John, the professor, and Mark. Most of all, he wrote about how much he loved his little sister. John arrived shortly afterward. "Where's Nat?" Mark shook his head. "We don't know." John looked at the notebook. "Has anyone tried calling him?" "His phone is here." John's expression changed. He immediately contacted the authorities and asked for help finding Nat. Hours passed. Then the family received the news. Nat had died. His family was left devastated. Mark broke down, unable to understand how he had lost both his sister and his brother. John stood silently, holding Nat's notebook. Their professor, who had always believed in Nat's potential, could only lower his head in sorrow. The family had lost another child. And this time, there would be no second chance to tell him how much he mattered.

The Final Letter

Several days later, John visited Nat and Ella's graves. He placed two bouquets of flowers beside them. He took out Nat's notebook and opened the page where Ella's drawing was kept. There were two people holding hands beneath a blue sky. One was a little girl with wings. The other was her older brother. Below the drawing, Ella had written: When I grow up, I want to fly with you, Kuya! John stared at the drawing. He remembered how Nat used to talk about his dreams. He wanted to graduate. He wanted to buy his family a house. He wanted Ella to become healthy. He wanted to give his siblings the childhood he never had. But the boy who spent his entire youth trying to save his family never got to see those dreams fulfilled. John wiped his tears and whispered: "You deserved so much more, Nat." He placed the notebook between the flowers. Then he looked toward the sky. Two siblings who once dreamed of a beautiful future were now gone, leaving behind a family that had failed to recognize their suffering until it was too late. And somewhere in the silence, the words of a little girl seemed to remain: 'Kuya, when I grow up, I'll take care of you.' But she never got the chance. And Nat never got the chance to see her grow up.
