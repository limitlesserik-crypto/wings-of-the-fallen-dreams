A TALE OF LOVE AND SACRIFICE

The Last Wings of Hope

CHAPTER 04

Days passed, and Ella's condition gradually worsened. Nat noticed that she was becoming weaker. She no longer played outside as often, and even walking around the house seemed exhausting for her. One afternoon, Nat came home carrying a small cake. "Ella!" His sister looked up from the sofa. "Kuya?" "Guess what day it is!" Ella thought for a moment. "My birthday?" Nat laughed. "No! It's the day my favorite little sister gets to eat cake." Ella giggled. "Kuya, you're so silly." He placed the cake on the table. "Come on. Let's celebrate." But Ella suddenly began coughing. Nat immediately approached her. "Ella? Are you okay?" She nodded weakly. "I'm just tired." Nat's smile slowly disappeared. He brought her some water and helped her rest. That evening, he took her to the hospital. After several examinations, the doctor asked Nat to speak with him privately. "Your sister's condition has become more serious," the doctor explained. Nat's heart sank. "What does that mean, Doctor?" "She needs consistent treatment and closer medical attention. We need to monitor her condition carefully." Nat swallowed. "How much will it cost?" The doctor explained the expected expenses. Nat listened, but every number felt heavier than the last. "Doctor, please. Is there anything else I can do?" "We will do everything we can. But she needs proper care and support." Nat nodded. "I understand." But he didn't know how he would afford it. When they returned home, Nat found his mother counting money. "Mom." She looked up. "What?" "Please. I need your help." His mother frowned. "What is it now?" "Ella's condition is getting worse. She needs treatment. I can't afford everything alone." His mother looked away. "I don't have money." Nat stared at her. "You don't have money?" "Yes!" "Then where did the money go?" His mother became angry. "Stop questioning me!" Nat's eyes began to fill with tears. "She's your daughter, Mom." His voice cracked. "She's not asking for expensive clothes or toys. She just wants to get better." His mother remained silent. Nat continued. "Do you know what she told me yesterday? She said she wanted to become a teacher someday. She wants to teach children and buy you a house." He wiped his tears. "She's still dreaming, Mom. She's still planning her future. But what happens if she doesn't get the chance?" His mother looked away. For a moment, the house became completely silent. Nat turned around and entered Ella's room. His sister was already asleep. He sat beside her and gently fixed her blanket. "Don't worry, Ella," he whispered. "Kuya will find a way." He looked at her small hands. "Just hold on a little longer." The next morning, Nat approached his professor. "Professor, may I ask if there are any additional scholarships or financial assistance programs?" The professor looked concerned. "Is this about your sister?" Nat nodded. "I need to earn more money for her treatment. I don't want to stop studying, but I don't know what else to do." The professor placed a hand on his shoulder. "Nat, I'll help you look for available assistance. But promise me you'll also take care of yourself." Nat nodded. For the first time in weeks, he felt that someone understood him. He returned home with a little hope. But hope, sometimes, arrives just before the world takes everything away.
